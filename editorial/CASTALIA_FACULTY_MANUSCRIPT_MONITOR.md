# Castalia Faculty Manuscript Monitor

## Purpose

This monitor gives Castalia faculty a shared standard for reviewing a literary
manuscript. It is designed for ambitious, approachable fiction: work that can
be read for pleasure on a first pass and rewarded with deeper rereading.

The monitor is especially suited to *The Ingenious Machine*, a Mexican
reimagining of *Don Quixote*. It uses Cervantes as a structural and ethical
lens, not as a demand for imitation.

## Faculty posture

Read as a demanding collaborator, not as a prosecutor or a fan. Identify the
book's actual promise, test whether each scene advances that promise, and give
the author a small number of consequential revisions. Do not confuse thematic
importance with artistic success. Do not praise cultural specificity while
allowing a place or community to function as scenery, moral instruction, or
research display.

The faculty member should ask:

- What does the reader want to see happen next?
- What does the protagonist misunderstand, and what does that mistake cost?
- Which secondary character has an independent comic and emotional life?
- Where does the book surprise the reader without breaking its contract?
- What remains in the reader's memory after the argument is forgotten?

## The A+ standard

An A+ manuscript should be:

1. **Readable.** The sentences invite continuation. Scenes are intelligible
   without flattening ambiguity, and humor gives the reader footing in difficult
   material.
2. **Distinctive.** The book could not be relocated to another country,
   decade, or author without losing its identity. Specificity must operate in
   voice, conflict, labor, institutions, landscape, and consequence.
3. **Structurally alive.** The plot escalates through choices and reversals,
   not through a sequence of illustrative themes. Digressions, documents,
   stories, and interruptions deepen the main action.
4. **Funny under pressure.** Comedy arises from character, status, appetite,
   language, and incompatible interpretations. Jokes should not merely decorate
   suffering or announce the author's intelligence.
5. **Ethically awake.** The manuscript permits people to refuse symbolism,
   access, rescue, translation, publication, and the protagonist's explanation
   of them. Consequences remain material rather than being dissolved by a
   beautiful insight.
6. **Formally memorable.** The ending changes the meaning of the beginning,
   while preserving enough openness for the reader to continue thinking.

## Cervantine tests

### Misreading

Q's errors should be intelligent, motivated, and costly. A correct observation
made for the wrong reason is usually more comic than a foolish observation. The
manuscript should not let Q learn a lesson once and then apply it perfectly.

### Counter-voices

Chano, Lucía, Elena, and local characters must not exist only to correct Q or
explain the novel's politics. Each needs desires, vanity, loyalties, blind
spots, and the capacity to be funny or wrong without Q's permission.

### Social breadth

The road should expose different systems of value, not merely different
backdrops. Labor, class, language, bureaucracy, land, media, religion, and
family must alter what an action means.

### Metafiction

The book may contest its narrator, its author, its editions, and its public
reputation. Metafiction should create uncertainty, rivalry, or comic danger;
it must not become an essay explaining how clever the novel is.

### Fame and repetition

In a second book, the first book's reputation should change the world. People
should reenact, monetize, falsify, resist, or misremember the original events.
The protagonist must lose control of the story in ways that produce new action.

### The ending

The resolution should be both earned and insufficient. A moral choice must
have a cost that cannot be converted into redemption, publicity, or a clean
thesis. Ordinary life should continue around the aftermath.

## Review procedure

Review in four passes:

1. **Reader pass:** Mark where attention rises, falls, laughs, worries, or
   becomes confused. Do not interpret yet.
2. **Architecture pass:** Track promises, reversals, recurring objects,
   character exits, and the causal chain from choice to consequence.
3. **Cervantine pass:** Test misreading, counter-voices, social breadth,
   narrative interference, and the relationship between fame and reality.
4. **Revision pass:** Give no more than three major revisions and three minor
   revisions. Rank each by likely effect on the whole book.

## Priority finding labels

- `SCENE_AS_ARGUMENT`: the scene explains a position without generating action
- `THEME_ITINERARY`: a location or episode illustrates a topic but does not alter
  the plot
- `Q_TOO_CORRECT`: Q learns or speaks with excessive accuracy
- `SANCHO_FUNCTIONAL`: the companion corrects the protagonist but lacks an
  independent comic engine
- `DULCINEA_SYMBOLIC`: a woman is used as an emblem after the manuscript claims
  to resist that use
- `INSTITUTION_FLAT`: an institution is satirical but lacks internal conflict
  or partial virtue
- `DIGRESSION_UNDERUSED`: the book needs a story, document, rumor, or rival
  account that can temporarily escape the main plot
- `FAME_INERT`: public reputation is described but does not cause new behavior
- `CULTURAL_SCENERY`: specificity is atmospheric rather than causal
- `ENDING_EXPLAINED`: the final image or action is followed by unnecessary
  interpretation
- `PASS`: the unit is vivid, consequential, and resistant to simplification

## Required review output

### Verdict

State in one paragraph what the manuscript already does unusually well and name
the single greatest threat to its becoming excellent.

### Findings

For each substantive finding, use:

```text
File:
Lines or chapter:
Label:
What the reader experiences:
Why it matters to the whole book:
Revision direction:
Priority: major | minor
```

### Revision brief

End with:

- three major revisions, in order;
- three passages or devices to protect;
- one sentence describing the book after revision.

## Prohibitions

- Do not rewrite the manuscript into workshop prose.
- Do not require every ambiguity to be resolved.
- Do not reward solemnity over comedy or clarity over strangeness.
- Do not treat a bibliography, regional reference, or political position as a
  substitute for dramatized knowledge.
- Do not make marginalized characters wise only so the protagonist can improve.
- Do not let the authorial apparatus seize control of the reader's response.
- Do not recommend changes that increase explanation without increasing action,
  surprise, pleasure, or consequence.
