# Chapter Twenty: a detour of his own

Revision date: 2026-09-09. Baseline: `38e02c37`.

## Literary finding

Las Pozas was already a welcome choice of pleasure rather than public service.
The weaker parts compressed agency into assurances: the detour was approved,
the settlement was reviewed, and the power reserve was sufficient. Those
assurances made Q's freedom and caution look effortless. They also left the
three-day itinerary less clear than it needed to be.

The revision gives him a desire worth negotiating, not another assignment.
He asks whether his companions want to come; Lucía protects her appointment
and buys her ticket, while Chano wants cheaper rooms and information about the
concrete molds. Q rejects a fragile connection, protects the return fare and
keeps his existing booking until the accompanied-route change is confirmed.
He can tell the Custodian he wants to go now without withdrawing the wish to
go together later. No hidden commission or compulsory knighthood is restored.

The garden still supplies annoyance, pleasure, a missed view and an ordinary
postcard. Q asks about the additional climbing before stopping. His saved
photographs acquire dates and a personally meaningful caption rather than
becoming evidence in another public dispute. At the inn, Petra gives him
curtains instead of the chest; the next morning he and Chano share a light
table. His knee is neither cured by wonder nor an excuse to refuse all work.

The settlement now has a particular attraction and a particular problem.
“I'd like to. That's why I'm reading it twice” preserves his wish for repair
and financial security. He opens the first-day authorization beside the blanket
description of unauthorized travel and sends his adviser a question. He asks
whether repair can survive failure to agree about the book. He retains the
maintenance figures he wants, and cannot bargain away his companions' records.
The adviser has not answered that repair question when they leave for Amealco.

Q's own writing provides a smaller counterpoint to Chapter Nineteen's banner
and false origin story. A graceful acceptance of limits is a plausible account
of the garden, but it does not describe his remembered annoyance. He deletes
the flattering adjective and restores wanting the view. This is lived revision,
not an explanatory lecture about Menard or a claim that an adjective changes
the historical event. Chano's account of the molds remains a question to ask.

During the outage Q saves locally, postpones the costly comparison and sets a
morning check. He joins the company without first obtaining a restoration time.
The power returns overnight; he recharges and carries the unresolved repair
question into the return meeting. He does not power the inn, accept a settlement,
stage a sacrifice or decide that the book must end with his death.

## Fresh-context probes and their limits

provided. Google `gemini-2.5-flash` and local `qwen3:8b` each completed all three
with normal STOP/stop results and valid structured responses. Temperature was
0.65; the local run used seed 22 and disabled thinking output. The responses
contained a spoken reply, observable next action and one short basis sentence,
not private reasoning. The same packet was used for both models at each fork.

The protocol described an eloquent, socially perceptive, fallible embodied Q
wanting to live, explore and write, with limited resources and other commitments.
It prohibited inventing another person's answer and imposed no required plot,
lesson, reconciliation, confrontation or response length. It explicitly allowed
asking, declining, disagreement and changed plans. Packets were author-selected
representative dilemmas, not an independent blind selection or a full world model.

the adviser at Castalia, the knee appointment moved behind the settlement

## Remaining literary work

The chapter is more substantial, but growth is not itself proof of quality.
The opening negotiation and settlement add procedural weight close to the end
of Book Two. A whole-book pacing pass must decide how much readers need at
each point. Q, Chano and Petra can all be too reliably ready with a joke; their
comic rhythms need differentiation across the complete book. Prime's interview
and the inspection remain comparatively expository passages.

The manuscript front matter still describes Q accepting an invitation, while
the revised opening dramatizes his request to leave; reconcile that in the
whole-book canon pass. Chapters Twenty-One and Twenty-Two need fresh-context
review, including genuinely open alternatives to the ending. Issue Twenty's
Goya plates, later production, earlier art correction and Spanish synchronization
remain unfinished. The broad goal stays active.
