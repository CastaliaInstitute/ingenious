## What changed

Sixteen built-in image-generation calls produced five initial scenes and successive
tests/edits. The first scenes preserved character details but inherited photographic
modeling, decorative background detail and cream substrate. Even explicit style
edits tended to preserve those underlying forms. Their hashes and complete prompts
remain in the manifest as rejected/intermediate evidence, not approved assets.

A fresh packet drawing using the actual Goya print as its only visual reference
made the useful change: elongated figures, expressive angular hands, a compressed
space and a broad dark tent place attention on the packet that is not taken.
The younger male Custodian has long dark hair and glasses; Q is absent. No letter
is opened, copied or destroyed. This is the strongest of the three selections.

The same fresh-drawing approach produced the two present-day scenes. Targeted edits
restored Q's full-length dark sleeves, removed borders and unsolicited lettering,
and clarified white reserves. His segmented narrow oval head, circular ears,
mechanical mouth, short jacket and right-knee support remain recognizable. The
Custodian's glasses are only on his head during the reunion and on his face during
the call. Pine silhouettes and a plain former data-center building replace the
irrelevant church/palm backdrop inherited by earlier attempts.

The present-day drawings still have more descriptive architectural texture and
naturalistic facial detail than Goya's most economical prints. They are selected
for scene-level review, not declared final artistic A+ work. Future prompts should
use these drawn studies for continuity without reverting to the old naturalistic
plates as rendering references. Avoid multiplying a failed style merely because
the scene mapping passes.
